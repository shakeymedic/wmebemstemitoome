Moving Beyond STEMI vs NSTEMI

The traditional STEMI/NSTEMI paradigm misses many acute coronary occlusions. This review advocates for a shift to an Occlusion MI (OMI) vs Non-Occlusion MI (NOMI) model. Recognising subtle ECG patterns is crucial for timely reperfusion and improved patient outcomes. This summary highlights key OMI patterns that don't meet traditional STEMI criteria.

Based on Ricci F, et al. Ann Emerg Med. 2025;85:330-340.

Clinical Implications & Takeaways

High Index of Suspicion:

Around 25-30% of 'NSTEMIs' have an occluded culprit artery. These NSTEMI-OMI patients have worse outcomes than NSTEMI-NOMI patients and benefit from urgent intervention.

Look Beyond Millimetres:

The morphology and context of ST-T changes are often more important than absolute millimetre criteria. Use serial ECGs and compare with previous recordings whenever possible.

Dynamic Process:

An ECG is a single snapshot. A patient's coronary artery may occlude, spontaneously reperfuse, and re-occlude. The ECG will change accordingly. Correlate with the patient's symptoms.

Discuss and Escalate:

If you see one of these high-risk patterns, even if STEMI criteria are not met, have a low threshold to discuss urgently with cardiology for consideration of early angiography.

This infographic is a summary and not a substitute for clinical judgment or local guidelines. Always correlate ECG findings with the patient's clinical presentation.
